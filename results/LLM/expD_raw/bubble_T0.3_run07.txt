[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r = 0.993) and vi–vm (r = 0.991). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site-related mass or molar descriptor), vm (molar volume), and vi (ionic volume) all scale with the size and mass of the constituent ions, so larger/heavier compositions tend to shift all three together.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} shows overlapping statistical information, with pm–vm = 0.993, vi–vm = 0.991, vi–pm = 0.984, pm–m = 0.982, and vm–m = 0.96; pd and va also overlap negatively (r = −0.806). This redundancy suggests these features are largely interchangeable in the model's view, but removal of any one cannot be justified from correlation alone without testing its unique contribution to the dielectric target.

[Design Implication] Given this structure, a compact informative set would pair one representative from the size/volume/mass cluster (e.g., vm or pm) with chemically distinct descriptors such as the electronegativity and ionization-energy features (blm, blsd, Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm) and the negatively correlated pd–va pair, since these capture complementary compositional and bonding information rather than duplicating the dominant size/mass signal.
