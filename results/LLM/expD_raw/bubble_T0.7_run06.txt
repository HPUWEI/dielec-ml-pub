[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r = 0.993) and vi–vm (r = 0.991). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site mass-related), vm (unit-cell/volume-related), and vi are all bulk structural/compositional descriptors that scale together with the overall atomic mass and packing of the perovskite composition.

[Redundancy] The features pm, vm, vi, and m form a tightly overlapping cluster (pm–vm 0.993, vi–vm 0.991, vi–pm 0.984, pm–m 0.982, vm–m 0.96), and pd–va are strongly anti-correlated at -0.806, so these variables carry largely shared statistical information. This overlap should be treated as a caution for model interpretation, not as proof that any single feature can be removed, since the correlations are pairwise and do not capture nonlinear or interaction effects used by the Gradient Boosting model.

[Design Implication] Given this structure, a potentially informative feature set would pair one representative from the redundant mass/volume cluster (e.g., vm or pm) with the anti-correlated pd–va axis, plus distinct descriptors such as the B-site electronegativity and radius features (blm, blsd, bvsdm) and A-site terms (sm, ssd, com, cosd, Em, Ev, Esd). This combination retains complementary mass/volume, size/electronegativity, and packing-related information rather than duplicating the pm–vm–vi–m signal, which is relevant for predicting the dielectric property (R² = 0.7472 ± 0.1632, RMSE = 15.4844 ± 6.7395).
