[Strong Correlations] The top two pairs are `pm`–`vm` (r = 0.993) and `vi`–`vm` (r = 0.991), with `vi`–`pm` (r = 0.984) close behind. These likely co-vary because molar mass, molar volume, and ionic volume all scale with the size and mass of the constituent ions, so compositions with heavier/larger ions tend to move together across all three descriptors.

[Redundancy] The cluster `pm`, `vm`, `vi`, and `m` (r = 0.982–0.993 among the first three, 0.96 for `vm`–`m`) carries heavily overlapping statistical information, as does the pair `pd`–`va` (r = −0.806). This overlap suggests the model may be splitting importance across near-duplicate size/volume descriptors, but removal should be confirmed by ablation or permutation testing rather than inferred from correlation alone.

[Design Implication] Given this structure, a compact informative set could combine one representative of the size/volume cluster (e.g., `pm` or `vm`) with features capturing orthogonal chemistry, such as the electronegativity and ionization-energy descriptors (`Em`, `Ev`, `Esd`, `bvsdm`, `devvm`) and the negatively correlated `pd`/`va` pair. Pairing a single size/volume term with `pd` (and/or `va`) may let the model capture both geometric and bonding/electronic contributions to the dielectric property without redundant inputs.
