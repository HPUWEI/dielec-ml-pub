[Key Features] The top features by SHAP mean |value| are **pd** (13.61), **pv** (12.00), and **blm** (2.73), with pd and pv an order of magnitude above the rest. In dielectric materials, **pd** and **pv** likely encode density-related and volume/polarizability-related descriptors (e.g., packing density and unit-cell/polar volume), which govern polarizability per unit volume, while **blm** plausibly reflects a B-site electronegativity or bonding-mean term influencing ionic/covalent character and hence polarizability.

[Model Insight] SHAP and raw importance largely agree at the top (pd, pv, blm lead both), but they diverge lower down: **via** ranks 4th by SHAP (1.89) yet only 10th by raw importance (0.0105), and **cosd** ranks 6th by SHAP (1.33) versus 11th by raw importance (0.0083), whereas **com** (0.0951) and **sm** (0.0589) rank 4th/5th by importance but 9th/12th by SHAP. This suggests the model leans on **com** and **sm** frequently for splits (possibly redundant with pd/pv), while **via** and **cosd** contribute larger per-instance effects, consistent with interaction or conditional dependence rather than dominant standalone drivers.

[Design Guidance] The concentration of SHAP mass in **pd** and **pv** suggests dielectric behavior in this dataset is associated mainly with density/volume-type descriptors, so compositions that shift these descriptors toward the high-SHAP region of the training space are worth prioritizing for screening. This is a model association only: it does not establish that tuning pd or pv causally improves the dielectric property, and any candidate must be validated experimentally, since the wide CV spread (R²=0.7472 ± 0.1632, RMSE=15.4844 ± 6.7395) and the pd/pv correlation with other features (e.g., blm, via, cosd) mean the model may be exploiting correlated proxies rather than the true physical driver.
