[Key Features] The top features by SHAP mean |value| are `pd` (13.61), `pv` (12.00), and `blm` (2.73), with `pd` and `pv` dominating by roughly 4-5x over the next feature. In dielectric materials, `pd` and `pv` likely encode packing-density and volume-related descriptors (e.g., A-site/B-site packing fraction and unit-cell volume), while `blm` plausibly represents a B-site bond-length mean, all of which influence polarizability and ionic displacement contributions to the dielectric response.

[Model Insight] SHAP and raw importance largely agree on the top three (`pd`, `pv`, `blm`), but they diverge notably for `via` (SHAP rank 4 at 1.89 vs. raw importance rank 10 at 0.0105), `cosd` (SHAP rank 6 at 1.33 vs. raw rank 11 at 0.0083), and `com` (raw rank 4 at 0.0951 vs. SHAP rank 9 at 0.65). This suggests `via` and `cosd` carry meaningful conditional/marginal contributions that tree split-gain underweights—consistent with interaction or masking effects—while `com`'s high split frequency may reflect redundant splits rather than large per-sample impact.

[Design Guidance] The model associates high `pd` and `pv` SHAP contributions with the dielectric target, so compositions or structures that shift these packing/volume descriptors fall in a region of high model sensitivity and are worth prioritizing for further screening. This is a model association only—not evidence that tuning `pd` or `pv` causally improves the dielectric property—and given the wide CV spread (R²=0.7472 ± 0.1632, RMSE=15.4844 ± 6.7395), any such hypothesis requires physical validation.
