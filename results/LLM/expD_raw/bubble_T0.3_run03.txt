[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991), followed closely by vi–pm (r=0.984). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site ionic mass or molar mass proxy), vm (molar volume), and vi (ionic volume) all scale with the size and mass of the constituent ions in the perovskite lattice, so heavier/larger cations tend to increase both mass and volume together.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} shows overlapping statistical information, with pm–m at r=0.982 and vm–m at r=0.96, and pd–va at r=-0.806 forms a separate negatively linked pair. This overlap suggests these features are largely interchangeable in the model's view, but removal of any one cannot be justified from correlation alone without testing its unique contribution to the dielectric target.

[Design Implication] Given this structure, a compact informative set could combine one representative from the mass/volume cluster (e.g., vm or pm) with pd and va as the negatively correlated pair, plus chemically distinct descriptors such as the electronegativity/ionization-energy features (emb, eab, epa, ieb) and GII. This would preserve compositional, size, and electronic information while limiting redundancy among the strongly co-varying mass/volume variables.
