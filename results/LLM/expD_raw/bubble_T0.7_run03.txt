[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site-related mass or molar parameter), vm (molar volume), and vi (ionic volume) all scale with the size and mass of the constituent ions in the perovskite-like lattice, so heavier/larger compositions tend to show simultaneously elevated mass and volume descriptors.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} carries heavily overlapping statistical information, with pm–vm=0.993, vi–vm=0.991, vi–pm=0.984, pm–m=0.982, and vm–m=0.96, and pd–va is the main negatively redundant pair at r=−0.806. These high intercorrelations mean the model's importance scores for these features are partly shared, so removal decisions should be validated by ablation or regularization rather than inferred from correlation alone.

[Design Implication] For predicting the dielectric property, a compact informative set could pair one representative from the mass/volume cluster (e.g., vm or pm) with chemically distinct descriptors such as the electronegativity/ionization-energy features (blm, Em, Ev, Esd) and the negatively correlated pd–va axis, since these capture complementary bonding, polarizability, and packing effects rather than duplicating the same size/mass signal.
