[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991), with pm–m (r=0.982) and vi–pm (r=0.984) close behind. These likely co-vary because pm (A-site mass), vm (unit-cell/ molar volume), vi (ionic volume), and m (molar mass) are all size- and composition-driven descriptors that scale together as the A-site cation and overall perovskite stoichiometry change.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} carries heavily overlapping statistical information (all pairwise |r|≥0.96), and pd–va (r=-0.806) is a notable inverse pair. This overlap suggests these features are largely interchangeable in a linear sense, but removal of any one should not be assumed safe without testing, since Gradient Boosting can still exploit small residual differences and non-linear interactions among them.

[Design Implication] Given this redundancy, the informative combination is likely one representative from the size/mass cluster (e.g., vm or pm) paired with complementary, less-correlated descriptors such as the electronegativity and ionization-energy features (blm, blsd, Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm) and the inversely related pd–va pair. Retaining both pd and va, or both a size descriptor and an electronic descriptor, should let the model capture the distinct geometric and electronic contributions to the dielectric property rather than duplicating the same variance.
