[Strong Correlations] The top two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site mass-related descriptor), vi (ionic volume-related term), and vm (molar/unit-cell volume term) all scale with the size and mass of the constituent ions, so heavier or larger ions tend to increase both mass and volume descriptors together.

[Redundancy] pm, vm, vi, and m form a tightly overlapping block (pm–vm 0.993, vi–vm 0.991, vi–pm 0.984, pm–m 0.982, vm–m 0.96), and pd–va is strongly negatively correlated (r=-0.806). This indicates substantial shared statistical information, but it does not by itself prove that any one feature can be removed without testing its unique contribution.

[Design Implication] Because the volume/mass block (pm, vm, vi, m) is largely redundant, a compact informative set could pair one representative from that block with the negatively correlated pd–va pair, while retaining other distinct descriptors such as blm, blsd, sm, ssd, com, cosd, Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm, devvm, via, pa, pv, lsd, asd, d, va, and GII to capture complementary dielectric-relevant chemistry and structure.
