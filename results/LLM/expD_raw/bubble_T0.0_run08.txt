[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991), with vi–pm close behind at r=0.984. These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site mass-related descriptor), vm (molar/unit-cell volume), and vi (ionic volume) all scale with the size and mass of the constituent ions, so heavier or larger ions tend to increase both mass and volume descriptors together.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} shows overlapping statistical information, with pm–m (r=0.982) and vm–m (r=0.96) also very high, and pd–va (r=-0.806) forms a separate strongly anti-correlated pair. This overlap suggests these features are largely redundant in a correlation sense, but removal of any single feature should not be assumed safe without testing its unique contribution to the Gradient Boosting model.

[Design Implication] Given this structure, a compact informative set could pair one representative from the mass/volume cluster (e.g., vm or pm) with pd and va to capture the negative pd–va relationship, while retaining chemically distinct descriptors such as the electronegativity and ionization-energy features (emb, eab, epa, ieb) and the remaining low-correlation features (blm, blsd, sm, ssd, com, cosd, Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm, devvm, via, pa, pv, lsd, asd, d, GII) to preserve complementary information for predicting the dielectric property.
