[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site mass-related descriptor), vi (ionic volume/valence-related descriptor), and vm (molar/unit-cell volume) all scale with the same compositional and structural size factors in perovskites, so heavier or larger constituent ions tend to produce larger cell volumes and related ionic-volume terms.

[Redundancy] The clusters {pm, vm, vi, m} (pm–vm 0.993, vi–vm 0.991, vi–pm 0.984, pm–m 0.982, vm–m 0.96) and {pd, va} (r=−0.806) carry overlapping statistical information, meaning several of these descriptors may be partially interchangeable in the model. However, correlation alone does not establish that any one of pm, vm, vi, m, pd, or va can definitely be removed, since each may still contribute unique nonlinear or interaction information to the Gradient Boosting model.

[Design Implication] Because the size/volume block (pm, vm, vi, m) is internally redundant, a compact and informative representation could retain one or two of these (e.g., vm plus vi) while pairing them with the inversely related pd–va pair, since pd and va capture a distinct dimension (r=−0.806) not explained by the volume cluster. Combining a representative volume/size term with pd or va, alongside the researcher-defined electronegativity and ionization-energy descriptors (emb, eab, epa, ieb), would give the model complementary rather than duplicated information for predicting the dielectric property.
