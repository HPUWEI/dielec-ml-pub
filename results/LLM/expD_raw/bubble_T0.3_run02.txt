[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site-related mass/parameter), vi (ionic volume/valence-related term), and vm (molar/unit-cell volume) all scale together with the size and identity of the constituent ions in the perovskite lattice, so larger or heavier A/B-site substitutions shift all three in tandem.

[Redundancy] pm, vm, vi, and m form a tightly intercorrelated block (pm–vm 0.993, vi–vm 0.991, vi–pm 0.984, pm–m 0.982, vm–m 0.96), and pd–va (r=-0.806) is a strong negative pair, so these features carry overlapping statistical information. This does not by itself prove that any one feature can be removed, since correlated features can still contribute distinct nonlinear signal to a Gradient Boosting model.

[Design Implication] Given this structure, a compact informative set would pair one representative of the pm/vm/vi/m size-volume block (e.g., vm) with pd and va to capture the strong negative pd–va contrast, while retaining chemically distinct descriptors such as the electronegativity/ionization-energy terms (blm, blsd, Em, Ev, Esd) and GII that are not part of the redundant block; this combination should preserve the compositional and ionic-size information most relevant to the dielectric property.
