[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r = 0.993) and vi–vm (r = 0.991). These likely co-vary because pm, vm, and vi are all size/volume-related descriptors of the perovskite unit cell, so a larger A- or B-site cation expands the cell volume and shifts the molar and ionic volume terms together.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} shows overlapping statistical information, with pm–vm = 0.993, vi–vm = 0.991, vi–pm = 0.984, pm–m = 0.982, and vm–m = 0.96; pd–va (r = -0.806) is a separate but notable inverse pair. These high correlations indicate redundancy, but removal of any single feature cannot be justified from correlation alone without checking model importance or ablation performance.

[Design Implication] For predicting the dielectric property, a compact but informative set could combine one representative from the size/volume cluster (e.g., vm or pm) with the inversely related pd–va pair, plus chemically distinct descriptors such as the electronegativity and ionization-energy features (emb, eab, epa, ieb) and the remaining low-correlation features like blm, blsd, sm, ssd, com, cosd, Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm, devvm, via, pa, pv, lsd, asd, d, va, and GII. This preserves the orthogonal chemical and structural information that the highly correlated size/volume features would otherwise duplicate.
