[Key Features] The top features by SHAP mean |value| are `pd` (13.61) and `pv` (11.99), followed by `blm` (2.73). In dielectric materials, `pd` and `pv` likely represent density- and volume-related descriptors (e.g., packing density and unit-cell or molar volume), while `blm` plausibly encodes a B-site bond-length or mass-related term tied to B-site electronegativity/radius chemistry.

[Model Insight] SHAP and raw importance largely agree at the top (`pd`, `pv`, `blm` rank 1–3 in both), but they diverge lower down: `via` is 10th in raw importance (0.010478) yet 4th in SHAP (1.885943), and `cosd` is 11th in importance (0.008339) but 6th in SHAP (1.334034), whereas `com` is 4th in importance (0.095113) but only 9th in SHAP (0.649655) and `sm` is 5th in importance (0.058921) but 12th in SHAP (0.453476). This suggests the model's split-based importance overstates `com`/`sm` usage while SHAP reveals stronger conditional contributions from `via` and `cosd`, consistent with interaction or redundancy effects among correlated descriptors.

[Design Guidance] The model associates high `pd` and `pv` with the dielectric target, with `blm`, `via`, and `cosd` contributing conditionally, so compositions/structures occupying regions of high `pd`/`pv` alongside favorable `blm` and `via`/`cosd` values may be worth prioritizing for screening. This is a model association only, not evidence that tuning `pd`, `pv`, or any single feature causally improves the dielectric property; the moderate nested CV spread (R²=0.7472 ± 0.1632, RMSE=15.4844 ± 6.7395) and likely feature correlations mean any such hypothesis requires physical validation.
