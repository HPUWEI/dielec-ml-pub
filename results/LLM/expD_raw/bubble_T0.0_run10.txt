[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site mass-related descriptor), vm (molar/unit-cell volume), and vi (ionic volume) all scale with the size and mass of the constituent ions, so larger, heavier ions tend to produce both greater ionic volume and greater molar volume.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} shows overlapping statistical information, with pm–vm=0.993, vi–vm=0.991, vi–pm=0.984, pm–m=0.982, and vm–m=0.96; pd and va also overlap negatively (r=-0.806). This indicates substantial multicollinearity, but removal of any single feature should not be decided from correlation alone—it requires testing whether the model's R²=0.7472 and RMSE=15.4844 degrade without it.

[Design Implication] Because pm, vm, vi, and m are nearly interchangeable, a model for the dielectric property could retain one representative (e.g., vm) plus complementary, less redundant descriptors such as the electronegativity/ionization-energy features (blm, blsd, Em, Ev, Esd) and the negatively correlated pd–va pair, which together may capture both compositional/volume effects and bonding/electronic effects relevant to the target.
