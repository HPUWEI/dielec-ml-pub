[Strong Correlations] The two strongest pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991), with vi–pm close behind at r=0.984. These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site-related mass or molar parameter), vm (molar volume), and vi (ionic volume) all scale with the same underlying ionic size and composition changes in the perovskite lattice, so larger ions raise mass, molar volume, and ionic volume together.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} carries heavily overlapping statistical information, since pm–vm=0.993, vi–vm=0.991, vi–pm=0.984, pm–m=0.982, and vm–m=0.96; pd and va also overlap inversely (r=-0.806). This redundancy suggests these features are largely interchangeable in information content, but it does not by itself prove that any one of pm, vm, vi, m, pd, or va can be removed without testing model performance.

[Design Implication] Because the size/volume block is so collinear, a compact informative set would pair one representative from {pm, vm, vi, m} with the inversely related pd–va axis, plus the composition/electronegativity descriptors (com, cosd, Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm, devvm) and the remaining structural/atomic terms (blm, blsd, sm, ssd, lsd, asd, GII, d, va, pa, pv, via) that are not captured by that size-volume cluster. This combination should retain the dielectric-relevant size, polarity, and bonding information while limiting the redundancy that inflates variance in the Gradient Boosting model (R²=0.7472 ± 0.1632, RMSE=15.4844 ± 6.7395).
