[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site mass-related descriptor), vi (ionic volume/valence-related term), and vm (molar/unit-cell volume term) all scale with the same compositional and structural size factors, so changes in cation identity or stoichiometry shift them together.

[Redundancy] pm, vm, vi, and m form a tightly overlapping block (pm–vm=0.993, vi–vm=0.991, vi–pm=0.984, pm–m=0.982, vm–m=0.96), and pd–va is strongly negatively correlated (r=-0.806). This indicates substantial shared statistical information among these features, but removal of any one feature should not be assumed from correlation alone and would require model-based validation.

[Design Implication] For predicting the dielectric property, a compact informative set could combine one representative from the pm/vm/vi/m size-related block (e.g., vm or pm) with pd and va to capture the strong negative pd–va contrast, while retaining chemically distinct descriptors such as the electronegativity/ionization-energy features (e.g., blm, blsd, Em, Ev, Esd) and GII to preserve complementary information.
