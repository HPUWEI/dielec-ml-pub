[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991); these likely co-vary because molar volume (vm) and the packing/mass descriptors pm and vi all scale with the size and mass of the constituent ions in the perovskite-type composition. A plausible materials-science reason is that larger B-site and A-site cations simultaneously increase unit-cell volume while shifting the mass- and volume-normalized descriptors pm and vi, so all three track the same underlying ionic-size trend.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} shows overlapping statistical information, with pm–m (r=0.982) and vm–m (r=0.96) indicating that mass and volume descriptors are largely redundant; pd and va also overlap negatively (r=-0.806). This does not mean any of these features can definitely be removed, since correlation alone does not establish that their non-linear contributions to the dielectric target are interchangeable.

[Design Implication] Given this redundancy, a compact informative combination would pair one representative of the mass/volume cluster (e.g., vm or pm) with features from less-correlated groups such as the electronegativity/ionization-energy descriptors (Em, Ev, Esd or the researcher-defined emb, eab, ieb) and the negatively correlated pd–va pair. This spans the size/mass, electronic-structure, and packing dimensions of the data while avoiding redundant duplication within the {pm, vm, vi, m} block.
