[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r = 0.993) and vi–vm (r = 0.991). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site mass-related descriptor), vi (ionic volume-related descriptor), and vm (molar/unit-cell volume) all scale with the size and mass of the constituent ions, so larger or heavier ions tend to increase both ionic and molar volume together.

[Redundancy] The cluster pm, vm, vi, and m shows overlapping statistical information, with pm–vm = 0.993, vi–vm = 0.991, vi–pm = 0.984, pm–m = 0.982, and vm–m = 0.96; pd and va also overlap negatively (r = -0.806). This redundancy suggests these features may be partially substitutable, but removal of any one feature cannot be justified from correlation alone and should be tested with the Gradient Boosting model's CV performance.

[Design Implication] Because the volume/mass cluster (pm, vm, vi, m) is highly collinear, a model for the dielectric property may benefit from retaining one representative from that group while pairing it with complementary features such as pd and va, which are strongly negatively correlated (r = -0.806) and thus encode distinct structural/chemical information. Including the B-site electronegativity and ionization-energy descriptors (blm, blsd, Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm) alongside a single volume/mass proxy could capture dielectric behavior without excessive redundancy.
