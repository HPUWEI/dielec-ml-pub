[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991), with vi–pm close behind at r=0.984. These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site ionic mass proxy), vm (molar/unit-cell volume), and vi (ionic volume) all scale together with the size and identity of the constituent ions in the perovskite lattice, so heavier or larger ions tend to occupy larger cell volumes.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} carries heavily overlapping statistical information, since pm–m (r=0.982) and vm–m (r=0.96) are also very high, and pd–va (r=-0.806) forms a separate strongly anti-correlated pair. This overlap suggests these features are largely interchangeable in the model's view, but removal of any one should be validated by ablation or feature-importance analysis rather than assumed from correlation alone.

[Design Implication] Because the size/volume block (pm, vm, vi, m) is internally redundant, a compact informative set could retain one representative from it (e.g., vm) alongside the anti-correlated pd–va pair, which encodes complementary packing/charge information. Pairing that reduced size/volume descriptor with the electronegativity and ionization-energy features (Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm, devvm) and the remaining structural/geometric terms (blm, blsd, sm, ssd, com, cosd, lsd, asd, GII) should preserve the chemical and structural diversity needed to predict the dielectric property.
