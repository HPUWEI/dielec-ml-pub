[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site mass-related descriptor), vm (molar/unit-cell volume), and vi (ionic volume) all scale with the size and mass of the constituent ions, so larger, heavier ions tend to produce both greater ionic volume and greater molar volume.

[Redundancy] pm, vm, vi, and m form a tightly overlapping block (pm–vm=0.993, vi–vm=0.991, vi–pm=0.984, pm–m=0.982, vm–m=0.96), and pd–va (r=-0.806) is a strongly anti-correlated pair; these features carry largely shared statistical information. This does not by itself prove that any one of them can be removed, since correlated features can still contribute distinct non-linear signal to a Gradient Boosting model.

[Design Implication] Given this redundancy, a compact and informative feature set would pair one representative from the volume/mass block (e.g., vm or pm) with the packing/density-related pd and va, and add chemically distinct descriptors such as the electronegativity and ionization-energy terms (blm, blsd, Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm) plus GII, rather than including pm, vm, vi, and m together.
