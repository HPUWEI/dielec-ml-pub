[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991), followed closely by vi–pm (r=0.984). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site ionic mass proxy), vm (molar/unit-cell volume) and vi (ionic volume) all scale with the size and mass of the constituent ions, so heavier, larger cations tend to produce both larger ionic volumes and larger cell volumes.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} carries heavily overlapping statistical information, with pm–m (r=0.982) and vm–m (r=0.96) reinforcing the same size/mass signal; pd–va (r=-0.806) is a separate but strong inverse pair. Because these are correlations among descriptors rather than causal tests, this does not by itself establish that any one of pm, vm, vi, or m can be removed without loss of predictive information.

[Design Implication] Given this redundancy, a compact but informative descriptor set would pair one representative of the size/mass cluster (e.g., vm or pm) with the inversely related pd–va pair, and then add the chemically distinct features that are not part of these correlation blocks — such as the electronegativity/ionization-energy descriptors (blm, blsd, Em, Ev, Esd) and the remaining structural/electronic terms (GII, lsd, asd, devvm, bvsdm) — so the model captures both geometric/volumetric and bonding/electronic contributions to the dielectric property.
