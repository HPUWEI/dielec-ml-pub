[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site-related mass/parameter), vi, and vm all scale with the same underlying compositional/structural descriptor—e.g., ionic mass and volume of the perovskite unit cell tend to increase together as heavier or larger cations occupy the A/B sites.

[Redundancy] pm, vm, vi, and m form a tightly overlapping block (pm–vm 0.993, vi–vm 0.991, vi–pm 0.984, pm–m 0.982, vm–m 0.96), and pd–va are strongly anti-correlated (r=-0.806). This indicates substantial shared statistical information among these features, but removal of any single feature cannot be justified from correlation alone—it requires testing whether the Gradient Boosting model's R²=0.7472 and RMSE=15.4844 degrade without it.

[Design Implication] Given this redundancy, the most informative feature combination for the dielectric property would pair one representative from the pm/vm/vi/m block (e.g., vm) with pd and va to capture the strong negative pd–va relationship (r=-0.806), while retaining chemically distinct descriptors such as bvsdm, devvm, and GII that are not part of the highly correlated set.
