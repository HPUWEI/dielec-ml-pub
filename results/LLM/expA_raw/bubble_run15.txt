[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991), followed closely by vi–pm (r=0.984). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site ionic mass or molar mass proxy), vm (molar volume), and vi (ionic volume) all scale with the size and mass of the constituent ions, so heavier/larger ions tend to increase both mass and volume in a coupled way.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} shows overlapping statistical information, with pm–m (r=0.982) and vm–m (r=0.96) also very high, and pd–va (r=-0.806) forms a separate strongly anti-correlated pair. This suggests substantial redundancy among the mass/volume descriptors and between pd and va, but correlation alone does not establish that any one feature can be removed without testing its unique contribution.

[Design Implication] Because pm, vm, vi, and m are nearly collinear, a model may benefit from retaining one representative from this group (e.g., vm) alongside complementary features such as the electronegativity/ionization-energy descriptors (blm, blsd, Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm) and the pd–va pair, which capture chemical and packing effects not encoded by the mass/volume cluster.
