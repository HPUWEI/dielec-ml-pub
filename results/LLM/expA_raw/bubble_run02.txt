[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991), followed closely by vi–pm (r=0.984) and pm–m (r=0.982). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site mass-related descriptor), vm (molar/unit-cell volume), vi (ionic volume), and m (mass) all scale with the size and identity of the constituent ions, so heavier or larger cations tend to produce both larger ionic volumes and larger unit-cell volumes.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} carries heavily overlapping statistical information, with pairwise |r| from 0.96 to 0.993, and pd–va (r=-0.806) forms a separate strongly anti-correlated pair. This overlap means these features are largely interchangeable in the model's view, but removal of any single feature cannot be justified from correlation alone, since each may still contribute unique non-linear signal to the Gradient Boosting model.

[Design Implication] Given this structure, a compact and informative feature set for predicting the dielectric property would combine one representative from the volume/mass cluster (e.g., vm or pm) with pd and va to capture the strong negative pd–va relationship, plus composition/electronegativity descriptors such as blm, blsd, sm, ssd, com, cosd, Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm, devvm, via, pa, pv, lsd, asd, d, and GII that are less collinear and may add independent predictive value.
