[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site-related mass/parameter), vi, and vm all scale with the same underlying compositional/structural descriptor—e.g., ionic mass and volume of the perovskite unit cell tend to increase together as heavier A/B-site cations are substituted.

[Redundancy] pm, vm, vi, and m form a tightly intercorrelated block (pm–vm 0.993, vi–vm 0.991, vi–pm 0.984, pm–m 0.982, vm–m 0.96), and pd–va are strongly anti-correlated (r=-0.806), so these features carry overlapping statistical information. This overlap suggests potential redundancy, but removal of any single feature cannot be justified from correlation alone—only from model-based importance or ablation testing.

[Design Implication] Given this structure, a compact informative set would pair one representative from the pm/vm/vi/m block (e.g., vm) with pd or va (retaining the pd–va anti-correlation signal, r=-0.806), plus the researcher-defined descriptors blm, blsd, sm, ssd, com, cosd, Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm, devvm, via, pa, pv, lsd, asd, d, va, and GII that are not part of the redundant block. This combination preserves both the dominant size/mass-related variance and the opposing pd–va trend relevant to dielectric response.
