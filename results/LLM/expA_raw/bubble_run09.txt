[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r = 0.993) and vi–vm (r = 0.991), followed closely by vi–pm (r = 0.984). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site mass-related descriptor), vm (molar/unit-cell volume), and vi (ionic volume) all scale with the size and mass of the constituent ions, so heavier or larger cations tend to increase both mass and volume in a correlated way.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} shows overlapping statistical information, with pm–m (r = 0.982) and vm–m (r = 0.96) also very high, and pd–va (r = -0.806) indicates a strong inverse overlap between those two. This suggests substantial multicollinearity among the mass/volume descriptors, though removal of any single feature should not be assumed from correlation alone.

[Design Implication] Because pm, vm, vi, and m are largely redundant, a more informative feature set for predicting the dielectric property would combine one representative from that mass/volume cluster (e.g., vm or pm) with complementary descriptors such as pd or va (which are strongly negatively correlated, r = -0.806) and the electronegativity/ionization features (blm, blsd, Em, Ev, Esd, ieb). This pairing captures both the size/mass contribution and the electronic/chemical contribution to the dielectric response.
