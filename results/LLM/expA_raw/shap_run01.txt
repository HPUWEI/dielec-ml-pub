[Key Features] The top features by SHAP mean |value| are `pd` (13.61), `pv` (12.00), and `blm` (2.73), with `pd` and `pv` dominating by roughly 4-5× over the next feature. In dielectric materials, `pd` and `pv` likely encode packing/density-related descriptors (e.g., A-site or B-site packing density and volume-related terms), while `blm` plausibly represents a B-site bond-length or electronegativity-related mean descriptor tied to B-site chemistry.

[Model Insight] SHAP and raw importance largely agree at the top (`pd`, `pv`, `blm` rank 1-2-3 in both), but they diverge lower down: `via` ranks 4th by SHAP (1.89) yet only 10th by raw importance (0.0105), and `cosd` ranks 6th by SHAP (1.33) versus 11th by importance (0.0083), whereas `com` ranks 4th by importance (0.0951) but 9th by SHAP (0.65) and `sm` ranks 5th by importance (0.0589) but 12th by SHAP (0.45). This suggests the tree model relies on `com`/`sm` for split frequency but their directional contribution per prediction is modest, while `via` and `cosd` contribute larger magnitude effects on individual predictions—consistent with interaction or context-dependent behavior rather than uniform additive dependence.

[Design Guidance] The model associates high `pd` and `pv` with the dielectric target, and the 80%-importance set (`pd`, `pv`, `blm`, `com`, `sm`, `Em`) is dominated by packing/volume and B-site chemistry descriptors, so compositions that jointly vary these descriptors are the most informative region to explore. This is a model association only—not evidence that tuning `pd` or `pv` causally improves the dielectric property—and any design hypothesis should be validated experimentally or with physics-based simulation before being treated as causal.
