[Key Features] The top features by SHAP mean |value| are `pd` (13.61), `pv` (12.00), and `blm` (2.73), with `pd` and `pv` dominating by roughly 4-5x over the next feature. In dielectric materials, `pd` and `pv` likely encode packing-density and volume-related descriptors (e.g., A-site/B-site packing fraction or molar volume terms), while `blm` plausibly represents a B-site bond-length or electronegativity-related mean descriptor tied to B-site cation chemistry.

[Model Insight] SHAP and raw importance largely agree at the top (`pd`, `pv`, `blm` rank 1-2-3 in both), but diverge below: `via` ranks 10th by raw importance (0.0105) yet 4th by SHAP (1.89), and `cosd` ranks 11th by importance (0.0083) but 6th by SHAP (1.33), while `com` and `sm` rank 4th/5th by importance (0.0951/0.0589) but drop to 9th/12th by SHAP (0.65/0.45). This suggests `via` and `cosd` carry context-dependent or interaction-driven signal that raw split-gain understates, whereas `com` and `sm` may contribute more through correlated redundancy with `pd`/`pv` than through unique marginal effects.

[Design Guidance] The model associates high `pd` and `pv` with the dielectric target, but this is a learned association within the training distribution, not evidence that manipulating packing density or volume causally improves the property; composition and processing changes that alter `pd`/`pv` would also alter `blm`, `com`, and other correlated descriptors, so any design hypothesis (e.g., exploring compositions where `pd`/`pv` co-vary with favorable `blm`) should be treated as a screening prior and validated experimentally rather than as a causal prescription.
