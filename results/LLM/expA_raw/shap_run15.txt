[Key Features] The top features by SHAP mean |value| are `pd` (13.61), `pv` (12.00), and `blm` (2.73), which likely represent packing density, packing volume, and B-site electronegativity (Matyonov-Batsanov), respectively, in dielectric materials. These three dominate the SHAP ranking, with `pd` and `pv` together accounting for a disproportionate share of model output magnitude relative to all other features.

[Model Insight] SHAP ranking and raw importance ranking largely agree at the top (`pd`, `pv`, `blm` are 1-2-3 in both), but diverge notably for `via` (SHAP rank 4, importance rank 10 at 0.010478) and `cosd` (SHAP rank 6, importance rank 11 at 0.008339), while `com` and `sm` rank higher in raw importance (4th and 5th) than in SHAP (9th and 12th). This suggests the model's split-based importance overstates `com` and `sm` due to frequent but low-magnitude splits, whereas `via` and `cosd` carry larger per-instance effects, possibly through interactions with the dominant `pd`/`pv` features.

[Design Guidance] The model associates high `pd` and `pv` with the largest SHAP contributions, and the six features `pd`, `pv`, `blm`, `com`, `sm`, `Em` jointly capture 80% of importance, so compositions varying these descriptors may be worth prioritizing for screening. However, this is a model association only—not evidence that altering packing density, packing volume, or B-site electronegativity will causally improve the dielectric property, since the model cannot separate correlation from causation and the wide CV variance (R²=0.7472 ± 0.1632) limits confidence in any single-feature design claim.
