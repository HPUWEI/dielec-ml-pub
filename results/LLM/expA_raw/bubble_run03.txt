[Strong Correlations] The two strongest pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991), followed closely by vi–pm (r=0.984). These likely co-vary because pm, vm, and vi are all size/volume-related descriptors of the perovskite unit cell, so a larger A- or B-site cation volume (vm) mechanically scales with molar/ionic volume and ionic volume terms (pm, vi).

[Redundancy] pm, vm, vi, and m form a tightly overlapping block (pm–vm=0.993, vi–vm=0.991, vi–pm=0.984, pm–m=0.982, vm–m=0.96), and pd–va (r=-0.806) is a strong inverse pair. These correlations indicate substantial shared statistical information, but they do not by themselves prove that any single feature is safe to remove, since non-linear or interaction effects in the Gradient Boosting model could still rely on each one.

[Design Implication] Given this structure, a compact but informative representation would pair one representative from the size/volume block (e.g., vm or pm) with the inversely related pd–va pair, and then add chemically distinct descriptors such as the electronegativity/ionization features (blm, blsd, Em, Ev, Esd, GII) that are not part of the r≥0.5 redundancy cluster. This combination retains the dominant geometric variance while preserving independent electronic information relevant to the dielectric property.
