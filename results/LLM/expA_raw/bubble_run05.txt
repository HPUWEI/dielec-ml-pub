[Strong Correlations] The two strongest pairs are pm–vm (r = 0.993) and vi–vm (r = 0.991), followed closely by vi–pm (r = 0.984) and pm–m (r = 0.982). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site-related mass/parameter), vm (unit-cell or molar volume), vi (ionic volume) and m (mass) all scale together with the size and identity of the constituent ions, so a larger/heavier A- or B-site cation tends to inflate both mass and cell volume in a coordinated way.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} carries heavily overlapping statistical information (all pairwise |r| ≥ 0.96), and pd–va (r = -0.806) is a strong inverse pair. This overlap suggests these features are largely interchangeable in a linear sense, but it does not by itself prove that any one of them can be removed without losing unique, non-linear information the Gradient Boosting model may exploit.

[Design Implication] Because pm, vm, vi and m are near-collinear, a compact informative set would pair one representative from that size/mass cluster (e.g., vm or pm) with the inversely related pd–va pair (r = -0.806) and with the remaining weakly correlated descriptors such as the electronegativity/ionization-energy features (blm, blsd, sm, ssd, com, cosd, Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm, devvm, via, pa, pv, lsd, asd, d, va, GII), which should add complementary rather than duplicated signal for predicting the dielectric property.
