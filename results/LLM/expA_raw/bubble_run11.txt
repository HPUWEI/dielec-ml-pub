[Strong Correlations] The two strongest pairs are pm–vm (r = 0.993) and vi–vm (r = 0.991), with vi–pm close behind at r = 0.984. These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site mass-related descriptor), vm (molar/unit-cell volume), and vi (ionic volume term) all scale with the ionic size and mass of the constituent cations, so heavier or larger ions inflate both mass and volume descriptors together.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} is statistically overlapping, since pm–m = 0.982 and vm–m = 0.96 in addition to the pairs above, and pd–va (r = -0.806) forms a separate inversely linked pair. This overlap means these features carry largely shared information, but removal of any single feature should not be asserted from correlation alone—it requires validation against the model's R²/RMSE (0.7472/15.4844) and feature-importance or ablation tests.

[Design Implication] Given this structure, a compact informative set would pair one representative from the size/mass cluster (e.g., vm or pm) with the inversely related pd–va pair, and then add chemically distinct descriptors such as the electronegativity and ionization-energy features (emb, eab, epa, ieb) plus GII, since these are not captured by the collinear mass/volume block and may explain residual dielectric variance.
