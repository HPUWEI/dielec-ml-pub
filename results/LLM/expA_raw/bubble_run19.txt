[Strong Correlations] The two strongest pairs are pm–vm (r = 0.993) and vi–vm (r = 0.991), followed closely by vi–pm (r = 0.984). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site-related mass or molar descriptor), vm (molar volume), and vi (ionic volume) all scale with the size and mass of the constituent ions, so a change in ionic radius or composition shifts all three together.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} shows overlapping statistical information, with pm–m (r = 0.982) and vm–m (r = 0.96) also very high, and pd–va (r = -0.806) is the only strong negative pair. This overlap suggests these features are largely collinear, but removal of any single feature cannot be justified from correlation alone and should be tested with feature-importance or ablation analysis.

[Design Implication] Because the size/volume/mass block (pm, vm, vi, m) is highly redundant, a compact informative set would pair one representative from that block (e.g., vm or pm) with the negatively correlated pd–va pair and with the remaining distinct descriptors such as blm, blsd, sm, ssd, com, cosd, Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm, devvm, lsd, asd, d, va, and GII. This combination retains both the ionic-size/volume information and the contrasting pd/va variation that may be relevant to the dielectric property.
