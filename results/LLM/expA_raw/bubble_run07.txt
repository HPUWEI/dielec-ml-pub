[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991), followed closely by vi–pm (r=0.984). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site molar-related mass or packing term), vm (molar volume), and vi (ionic volume) all scale with the same unit-cell size and cation-packing geometry, so a larger molar volume mechanically implies larger ionic and mass-related descriptors.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} carries heavily overlapping statistical information (pm–vm=0.993, vi–vm=0.991, vi–pm=0.984, pm–m=0.982, vm–m=0.96), and pd–va (r=-0.806) is a separate strongly anti-correlated pair. This overlap suggests these features are largely interchangeable in the model's view, but correlation alone does not prove that any single one can be removed without testing its unique contribution (e.g., via permutation importance or ablation).

[Design Implication] Because the size/volume block {pm, vm, vi, m} is nearly collinear, a compact and informative descriptor set would retain one representative from that block (e.g., vm) alongside complementary features that are not redundant with it—such as the electronegativity/ionization-energy terms (Em, Ev, Esd, or the B-site electronegativity descriptors) and the anti-correlated pd–va pair—so the model captures both geometric/packing and chemical-bonding drivers of the dielectric property rather than duplicating size information.
