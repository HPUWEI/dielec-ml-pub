[Key Features] The top features by SHAP mean |value| are `pd` (13.61), `pv` (12.00), and `blm` (2.73), with `pd` and `pv` dominating by roughly 4-5x over the next feature. In dielectric materials, `pd` and `pv` likely represent density-related or packing/volume descriptors (e.g., A-site or B-site packing density and volume), while `blm` plausibly encodes a B-site bond-length or electronegativity-related mean term, all of which influence polarizability and dielectric response.

[Model Insight] SHAP and raw importance largely agree at the top (`pd`, `pv`, `blm` rank 1-3 in both), but they diverge lower down: `via` ranks 10th in raw importance (0.010478) yet 4th in SHAP (1.886), and `cosd` ranks 11th in importance (0.008339) but 6th in SHAP (1.334), while `com` ranks 4th in importance (0.095113) but only 9th in SHAP (0.650). This suggests `via` and `cosd` carry strong but context-dependent (interaction-driven) effects that raw split-based importance understates, whereas `com`'s high importance may partly reflect frequent splitting rather than consistent marginal contribution.

[Design Guidance] The model associates high `pd` and `pv` with the dielectric target, but this is a learned association, not evidence of causality—compositional or structural changes that raise `pd`/`pv` may or may not improve the property. A cautious, model-based hypothesis is that compositions simultaneously tuning `pd`, `pv`, and `blm` (rather than any single feature) fall in regions the model predicts favorably, and this should be validated experimentally before any design claim.
