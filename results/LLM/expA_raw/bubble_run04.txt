[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site mass-related descriptor), vi (ionic volume/valence-related term), and vm (molar/unit-cell volume term) all scale with the same compositional and structural size factors, so heavier or larger constituent ions tend to increase both mass and volume descriptors together.

[Redundancy] pm, vm, vi, and m form a tightly overlapping block (pm–vm=0.993, vi–vm=0.991, vi–pm=0.984, pm–m=0.982, vm–m=0.96), and pd–va (r=-0.806) is another strongly linked pair. This indicates substantial shared statistical information among these features, but removal of any one feature cannot be justified from correlation alone and should be tested with importance or ablation analysis.

[Design Implication] For predicting the dielectric property, a compact informative set could combine one representative from the size/volume block (e.g., vm or pm) with the inversely related pd–va pair (pd and/or va), plus chemically distinct descriptors such as the electronegativity and ionization-energy features (blm, blsd, Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm, devvm) that are not part of the strong-correlation clusters.
