[Key Features] The three most important features by SHAP mean |value| are `pd` (13.61), `pv` (12.00), and `blm` (2.73), which likely reflect A-site/B-site ionic packing density, unit-cell volume or polarization-related volume, and B-site electronegativity (Matyonov-Batsanov), respectively, all of which can influence dielectric polarizability and permittivity. `pd` and `pv` dominate both SHAP and raw importance, indicating that structural/volumetric descriptors are the primary learned correlates of the dielectric target.

[Model Insight] The SHAP ranking largely agrees with the raw importance ranking for the top three (`pd`: 0.3121 importance, 13.61 SHAP; `pv`: 0.1785, 12.00; `blm`: 0.1514, 2.73), but diverges for `via` (0.0105 importance, 1.89 SHAP), `cosd` (0.0083, 1.33), and `Ev` (0.0429, 1.40), which have modest raw importance yet relatively large SHAP contributions. This suggests the model relies on `pd` and `pv` as dominant main effects while `via`, `cosd`, and `Ev` contribute through interactions or context-dependent splits that raw split-based importance understates.

[Design Guidance] Because `pd` and `pv` are strongly associated with the model output in both SHAP (13.61 and 12.00) and raw importance (0.3121 and 0.1785), compositions or structures that vary these descriptors may be worth prioritizing for dielectric screening, but this is a model association and not evidence that changing `pd` or `pv` causally enhances the dielectric property. The weaker but non-negligible SHAP signals for `via` (1.89) and `Ev` (1.40) further suggest that interaction-aware descriptors should be retained in follow-up models rather than pruning to the top raw-importance features alone.
