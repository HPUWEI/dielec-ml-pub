[Strong Correlations] The two strongest pairs are pm–vm (r = 0.993) and vi–vm (r = 0.991), followed closely by vi–pm (r = 0.984) and pm–m (r = 0.982). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site mass-related descriptor), vm (molar/unit-cell volume), vi (ionic volume) and m (mass) all scale with the size and identity of the constituent ions, so heavier or larger cations tend to produce both larger ionic volumes and larger unit-cell volumes.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} carries heavily overlapping statistical information (all pairwise |r| ≥ 0.96), and pd–va (r = -0.806) is a strongly anti-correlated pair. This redundancy suggests these features are largely interchangeable in the model's view, but removal of any single feature should be confirmed by ablation or permutation importance rather than inferred from correlation alone.

[Design Implication] Because the volume/mass block (pm, vm, vi, m) is nearly collinear, a compact informative set could pair one representative from that block (e.g., vm) with the independent anti-correlated pair pd and va, and then add chemically distinct descriptors such as the electronegativity/ionization-energy features (blm, blsd, Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm) to capture dielectric behavior beyond geometric scaling.
