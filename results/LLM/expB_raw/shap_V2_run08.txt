[Key Features] The top features by SHAP mean |value| are `pd` (13.61), `pv` (12.00), and `blm` (2.73), with `pd` and `pv` dominating by roughly 5–10× over the next tier. In dielectric materials, `pd` and `pv` likely correspond to packing-density and pore-volume descriptors that govern polarizability and dipolar response, while `blm` (B-site electronegativity–Matyonov-Batsanov) reflects B-site bonding character and hence ionic/covalent contributions to the dielectric constant.

[Model Insight] The raw importance ranking and SHAP ranking largely agree at the top (`pd` 0.312 vs 13.61; `pv` 0.179 vs 12.00; `blm` 0.151 vs 2.73), but they diverge for `via` (raw importance 0.0105, rank 10; SHAP 1.89, rank 4) and `cosd` (raw 0.0083, rank 11; SHAP 1.33, rank 6), while `com` (raw 0.0951, rank 4; SHAP 0.650, rank 9) and `sm` (raw 0.0589, rank 5; SHAP 0.453, rank 12) drop under SHAP. This suggests the model's split-based importance over-credits `com` and `sm` through interactions with the dominant `pd`/`pv` pair, whereas `via` and `cosd` carry more consistent, directionally stable contributions across samples.

[Design Guidance] The strong SHAP association of `pd` and `pv` with the dielectric target, together with the secondary role of `blm`, suggests a cautious hypothesis that compositions simultaneously achieving high packing density (`pd`) and low pore volume (`pv`) while tuning B-site electronegativity (`blm`) may tend to fall in favorable dielectric regimes—but this is a model association, not evidence that manipulating `pd`, `pv`, or `blm` will causally improve the dielectric property, and the wide CV spread (R²=0.7472 ± 0.1632, RMSE=15.4844 ± 6.7395) warrants experimental validation before any design claim.
