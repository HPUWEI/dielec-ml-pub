[Strong Correlations] The two strongest feature pairs are ('pm', 'vm') at r=0.993 and ('vi', 'vm') at r=0.991, with ('vi', 'pm') close behind at r=0.984. These co-vary plausibly because 'pm' (A-site/B-site-related mass or molar parameter), 'vm' (molar volume), and 'vi' (ionic volume) all scale with the same ionic size and packing chemistry, so heavier/larger constituent ions inflate both mass- and volume-type descriptors together.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} shares heavily overlapping statistical information (all pairwise |r|≥0.96), and ('pd', 'va') at r=-0.806 forms a separate strongly anti-correlated pair. This overlap means these features are largely interchangeable in a Gradient Boosting model, but correlation alone does not establish that any one of them is removable, since tree splits can exploit small residual differences among them.

[Design Implication] Given this structure, a compact informative combination would retain one representative from the {pm, vm, vi, m} cluster (e.g., 'vm') plus one from the ('pd', 'va') pair (e.g., 'pd'), and pair these with the weakly correlated descriptors such as 'blm', 'blsd', 'sm', 'ssd', 'com', 'cosd', 'Em', 'Ev', 'Esd', 'bvsdm', 'devvm', 'via', 'pa', 'pv', 'lsd', 'asd', 'd', 'va', and 'GII' to predict the dielectric property without redundant collinear inputs.
