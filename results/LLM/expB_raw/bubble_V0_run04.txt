[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991), followed closely by vi–pm (r=0.984). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site ionic mass or molar mass descriptor), vm (molar volume), and vi (ionic volume) all scale with the size and mass of the constituent ions, so heavier/larger ions tend to increase both molar mass and molar/ionic volume together.

[Redundancy] pm, vm, vi, and m form a tightly intercorrelated block (pm–vm 0.993, vi–vm 0.991, vi–pm 0.984, pm–m 0.982, vm–m 0.96), and pd–va are strongly negatively correlated (r=-0.806), indicating overlapping statistical information within each group. This overlap suggests redundancy, but removal of any single feature cannot be justified from correlation alone, since each may still contribute unique variance to the dielectric target.

[Design Implication] Given this structure, a compact but informative feature set could pair one representative from the pm/vm/vi/m mass–volume block (e.g., vm) with one from the pd/va pair (e.g., pd), and add the less-correlated descriptors such as the B-site electronegativity/radius terms (blm, blsd, sm, ssd, Em, Ev, Esd) and GII to capture compositional and bonding effects relevant to the dielectric property.
