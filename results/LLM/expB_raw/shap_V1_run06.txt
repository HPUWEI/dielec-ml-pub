[Key Features] The three most important features by SHAP mean |value| are **pd** (13.61), **pv** (12.00), and **blm** (2.73), with **pd** and **pv** dominating by roughly 4–5× over the next feature. In dielectric materials, **pd** and **pv** likely reflect packing/volume-related descriptors (e.g., density- or volume-normalized polarization terms), while **blm** (B-site electronegativity–Matyonov-Batsanov) reflects B-site bonding character and covalency, which influences polarizability and hence the dielectric response.

[Model Insight] The SHAP ranking largely agrees with the raw importance ranking at the top (**pd** 0.312, **pv** 0.179, **blm** 0.151), but diverges lower down: **via** ranks 10th in raw importance (0.0105) yet 4th in SHAP (1.89), and **cosd** ranks 11th in importance (0.0083) but 6th in SHAP (1.33), whereas **com** ranks 4th in importance (0.095) but only 9th in SHAP (0.65) and **sm** ranks 5th in importance (0.059) but 12th in SHAP (0.45). This implies the model's split-based importance overstates **com** and **sm** (likely used in many low-impact splits or interactions), while **via** and **cosd** carry larger per-instance effects, suggesting the model relies on interaction or conditional effects not captured by raw gain importance.

[Design Guidance] The strong SHAP association of **pd** (13.61) and **pv** (12.00), together with **blm** (2.73), suggests that compositions simultaneously optimizing packing/volume descriptors and B-site electronegativity character tend to appear in higher-dielectric regions of the training data. This is a model association only—it does not establish that tuning **pd**, **pv**, or **blm** will causally enhance the dielectric property, and any design hypothesis should be validated experimentally given the wide CV spread (R²=0.7472 ± 0.1632, RMSE=15.4844 ± 6.7395).
