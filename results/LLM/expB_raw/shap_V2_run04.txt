[Key Features] The top features by SHAP mean |value| are `pd` (13.61) and `pv` (11.99), followed by `blm` (2.73); `pd` and `pv` likely correspond to A-site/B-site ionic polarizability or polarizability-volume terms that govern dielectric permittivity, while `blm` (B-site electronegativity–Matyonov-Batsanov) reflects B-site bonding character. The remaining SHAP contributors, `via` (1.89), `Ev` (1.40), and `cosd` (1.33), are far smaller, indicating the model's dielectric prediction is dominated by the polarizability-related descriptors.

[Model Insight] Raw importance ranks `pd` (0.312), `pv` (0.179), and `blm` (0.151) at the top, matching SHAP for those three, but diverges for `via` and `cosd`, which rank 10th (0.0105) and 11th (0.0083) by importance yet 4th (1.89) and 6th (1.33) by SHAP, while `com` ranks 4th by importance (0.095) but only 9th by SHAP (0.650). This suggests `via` and `cosd` carry strong local or interaction-driven effects that split-based importance understates, whereas `com`'s high importance may partly reflect redundant splits shared with `pd`/`pv`.

[Design Guidance] The association of high `pd` and `pv` with the dielectric target suggests that compositions with larger polarizability-related descriptor values tend to appear in higher-permittivity regions of the training data, but this is a model association, not evidence that tuning `pd` or `pv` will causally raise permittivity. Any design hypothesis based on `pd`, `pv`, and `blm` should be treated as a screening heuristic to be validated experimentally, since the nested CV spread (R²=0.7472 ± 0.1632, RMSE=15.4844 ± 6.7395) indicates substantial uncertainty and possible confounding among correlated features.
