[Dominance] Six of the 24 features account for 80% of the predictive importance: pd (0.312094), pv (0.178505), blm (0.151432), com (0.095113), sm (0.058921), and Em (0.048412). Importance is strongly concentrated, with pd and pv alone contributing roughly 49% and the top three (pd, pv, blm) contributing about 64%.

[Physical Meaning] pd and pv are the dominant predictors, plausibly because they encode packing/volume-related descriptors that govern polarizability and dipole density, which directly influence dielectric response. blm, the third-ranked feature, is a B-site electronegativity descriptor (Matyonov-Batsanov), and B-site chemistry affects cation-oxygen bonding and hence ionic/electronic polarizability. com and sm, while smaller in importance, likely capture compositional and structural/mean-size effects that modulate the dielectric constant.

[Simplification] The long-tail features with the lowest importances—pm (0.000758), asd (0.00096), GII (0.001393), ssd (0.00167), Esd (0.003693), and d (0.004818)—are candidates for further investigation as lower-priority variables. Whether they can be removed without degrading the R²=0.7472 ± 0.1632 performance should be tested by a separate retraining/ablation experiment rather than assumed.
