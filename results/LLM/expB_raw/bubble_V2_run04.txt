[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991), with vi–pm close behind (r=0.984). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site-related mass or molar parameter), vm (molar volume), and vi (ionic volume) all scale with the same underlying ionic size/chemistry, so larger constituent ions simultaneously raise molar volume and ionic volume.

[Redundancy] pm, vm, vi, and m form a tightly overlapping block (pm–vm=0.993, vi–vm=0.991, vi–pm=0.984, pm–m=0.982, vm–m=0.96), and pd–va is strongly negatively correlated (r=-0.806), so these features carry largely shared statistical information. However, correlation alone does not establish that any of pm, vm, vi, m, pd, or va can be removed, since each may still contribute unique variance to the dielectric target.

[Design Implication] Because pm, vm, vi, and m are nearly collinear, a model predicting the dielectric property should retain only one or two of them (e.g., vm plus m) and pair them with the negatively correlated pd–va axis, which encodes a distinct structural/electronic trade-off. Adding features outside these correlated blocks—such as the researcher-defined emb, arb, eab, epa, and ieb—would likely improve coverage of the dielectric response beyond what the redundant size/volume cluster provides.
