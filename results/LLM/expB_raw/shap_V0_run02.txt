[Key Features] The top features by SHAP mean |value| are `pd` (13.61) and `pv` (11.99), followed distantly by `blm` (2.73). In dielectric materials, `pd` and `pv` likely encode A-site/B-site ionic polarizability or polarization-related volume/charge descriptors, while `blm` (B-site electronegativity–Matyonov-Batsanov) reflects B-site bonding covalency/ionicity character.

[Model Insight] SHAP and raw importance largely agree on the top three (`pd`, `pv`, `blm`), but they diverge notably for `via` (SHAP rank 4 at 1.89 vs. importance rank 10 at 0.0105), `cosd` (SHAP rank 6 at 1.33 vs. importance rank 11 at 0.0083), and `com` (SHAP rank 9 at 0.65 vs. importance rank 4 at 0.0951). This suggests the model's split-based importance for `com` may be inflated by frequent but low-effect splits, whereas `via` and `cosd` carry larger per-instance effects, possibly through interactions with the dominant `pd`/`pv` features.

[Design Guidance] The model associates high `pd` and `pv` with the largest SHAP contributions, so compositions where these descriptors vary strongly are where predictions are most sensitive; this is a model association, not evidence that tuning `pd`/`pv` causally improves the dielectric property. Any design hypothesis—e.g., exploring A-site/B-site substitutions that shift `pd`/`pv` while holding `blm` moderate—should be treated as a screening heuristic and validated experimentally, since SHAP magnitudes reflect model dependence rather than physical causality.
