[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r = 0.993) and vi–vm (r = 0.991). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site-related mass or molar descriptor), vm (molar volume), and vi (ionic volume) all scale with the size and mass of the constituent ions in the perovskite-like lattice, so heavier or larger ions tend to increase both mass and volume descriptors together.

[Redundancy] The cluster of pm, vm, vi, and m shows overlapping statistical information, with pm–vm = 0.993, vi–vm = 0.991, vi–pm = 0.984, pm–m = 0.982, and vm–m = 0.96; pd and va also overlap negatively (r = -0.806). This redundancy suggests these features are not statistically independent, but it does not by itself prove that any one feature can be removed without testing its unique contribution to the dielectric target.

[Design Implication] Because the volume/mass block (pm, vm, vi, m) is highly collinear, a model for the dielectric property may benefit from retaining one or two representatives from that group while pairing them with less redundant features such as the electronegativity and ionization-energy descriptors (blm, blsd, Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm, devvm) and the negatively correlated pd–va pair. This combination could capture both compositional/electronic effects and the size–volume effects that are known to influence dielectric behavior, while limiting redundant inputs.
