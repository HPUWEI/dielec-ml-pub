[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991), with vi–pm also nearly as high (r=0.984). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site mass-related descriptor), vm (molar/unit-cell volume), and vi (ionic volume) all scale with the size and mass of the constituent ions, so heavier or larger ions simultaneously raise mass and volume descriptors.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} shows overlapping statistical information, with pm–m (r=0.982) and vm–m (r=0.96) indicating that mass and volume descriptors are largely redundant with one another; pd–va (r=-0.806) is a separate, strongly anti-correlated pair. Correlation alone does not establish that any of pm, vm, vi, or m can be removed, since each may still contribute unique non-linear signal to the Gradient Boosting model.

[Design Implication] Because pm, vm, vi, and m are mutually redundant, a compact informative set could retain one representative from that cluster (e.g., vm) alongside pd and va to capture the strong negative packing/density relationship, while preserving chemically distinct descriptors such as Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm, devvm, and GII that are not strongly correlated with the volume/mass group.
