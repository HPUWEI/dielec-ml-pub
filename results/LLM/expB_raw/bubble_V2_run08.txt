[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991), with vi–pm also at r=0.984. These likely co-vary because pm, vm, and vi all scale with the same underlying compositional/structural descriptor—e.g., molar volume, molar mass, and ionic volume/valence-related terms tend to increase together as the B-site or overall formula unit becomes larger and heavier.

[Redundancy] pm, vm, vi, and m form a tightly overlapping block (pm–vm 0.993, vi–vm 0.991, vi–pm 0.984, pm–m 0.982, vm–m 0.96), and pd–va are strongly negatively correlated (r=-0.806), so these features carry largely shared statistical information. However, correlation alone does not establish that any of pm, vm, vi, m, pd, or va can definitely be removed, since each may still contribute unique nonlinear or interaction information to the Gradient Boosting model.

[Design Implication] Given this correlation structure, a potentially informative combination would pair one representative from the pm/vm/vi/m cluster (e.g., vm or pm) with pd and va to capture the strong negative pd–va axis, while retaining chemically distinct descriptors such as the electronegativity/ionization-energy features (blm, blsd, Em, Ev, Esd, GII) that are not part of the |r|≥0.5 list. This would preserve complementary variance for predicting the dielectric property rather than feeding multiple near-duplicate size/mass descriptors.
