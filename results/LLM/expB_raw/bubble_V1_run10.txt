[Strong Correlations] The two strongest correlated pairs are (pm, vm) at r=0.993 and (vi, vm) at r=0.991, both involving the molar volume feature vm. This co-variation is plausible because pm (likely a mass- or packing-related descriptor) and vi (likely a volume- or ion-size-related descriptor) both scale with the unit-cell volume vm in perovskite-type dielectrics, so they track the same structural expansion.

[Redundancy] Features pm, vm, vi, and m form a tightly overlapping block (pm–vm 0.993, vi–vm 0.991, vi–pm 0.984, pm–m 0.982, vm–m 0.96), and pd–va is strongly anti-correlated at -0.806, so these variables carry largely shared statistical information. Correlation alone does not establish that any of pm, vm, vi, m, pd, or va can be removed, since each may still contribute unique non-linear signal to the Gradient Boosting model.

[Design Implication] Given this structure, a compact informative combination would pair one representative of the pm/vm/vi/m volume block (e.g., vm) with pd or va from the anti-correlated pair, plus the composition/electronegativity descriptors blm, blsd, sm, ssd, com, cosd, Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm, devvm, lsd, asd, GII, and the remaining pa, pv, via, d, va, which are not part of the |r|≥0.5 clusters. This retains the volume and the pd–va contrast while avoiding feeding the model four near-duplicate volume features.
