[Key Features] The top features by SHAP mean |value| are `pd` (13.61), `pv` (12.00), and `blm` (2.73). In dielectric materials, `pd` and `pv` likely represent density-related or polarization/volume descriptors tied to packing and polarizability, while `blm` plausibly encodes a B-site bond-length or mass-related term influencing lattice polarizability.

[Model Insight] SHAP and raw importance largely agree at the top (`pd`, `pv`, `blm` rank 1-2-3 in both), but they diverge lower down: `via` is 10th by importance (0.0105) yet 4th by SHAP (1.89), and `cosd` is 11th by importance (0.0083) but 6th by SHAP (1.33), whereas `com` is 4th by importance (0.0951) but only 9th by SHAP (0.65) and `sm` is 5th by importance (0.0589) but 12th by SHAP (0.45). This suggests the model's split-based importance overstates `com` and `sm` (likely used frequently in shallow splits or interacting with `pd`/`pv`), while `via` and `cosd` carry larger conditional/marginal contributions than their split counts imply.

[Design Guidance] The dominance of `pd` and `pv` (combined SHAP ≈ 25.6, versus 2.73 for the next feature) suggests the model associates dielectric behavior most strongly with whatever structural/electronic property these encode, so compositions varying `pd`/`pv` while holding `blm`, `com`, and `sm` fixed would be the most informative to test. This is a model association only—it does not establish that tuning `pd` or `pv` causally improves the dielectric property, and the wide CV spread (R²=0.7472 ± 0.1632, RMSE=15.4844 ± 6.7395) warrants experimental validation before any design claim.
