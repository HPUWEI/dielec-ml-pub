[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991), with vi–pm also extremely high at r=0.984. These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site ionic mass proxy), vm (molar/unit-cell volume), and vi (ionic volume) all scale together with the ionic size and mass of the constituent cations, so heavier/larger ions tend to occupy larger volumes in the perovskite lattice.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} shows overlapping statistical information, with pm–vm=0.993, vi–vm=0.991, vi–pm=0.984, pm–m=0.982, and vm–m=0.96; pd and va are also strongly inversely related (r=-0.806). This overlap suggests these features are largely collinear, but removal should not be decided from correlation alone because each may still contribute unique nonlinear or interaction information to the Gradient Boosting model.

[Design Implication] For predicting the dielectric property, a compact informative set could combine one representative from the size/mass cluster (e.g., vm or pm) with pd and va to capture the strong negative pd–va relationship, plus chemically distinct descriptors such as the electronegativity/ionization-energy features (blm, blsd, Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm) and GII. This would preserve the dominant size/volume and A/B-site chemistry signals while reducing redundancy among pm, vm, vi, and m.
