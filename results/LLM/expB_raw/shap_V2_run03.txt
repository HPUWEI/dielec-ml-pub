[Key Features] The top features by SHAP mean |value| are `pd` (13.61), `pv` (12.00), and `blm` (2.73), with `pd` and `pv` dominating by roughly 5–10× over the next tier. In dielectric materials, `pd` and `pv` plausibly encode polarization-related descriptors (e.g., polarizability/dipole contributions), while `blm` likely reflects a B-site bond-length or mass-related structural term.

[Model Insight] Raw importance ranks `pd` (0.312), `pv` (0.179), and `blm` (0.151) at the top, matching SHAP for the top three, but diverges below: `via` ranks 10th in importance (0.0105) yet 4th in SHAP (1.886), and `cosd` ranks 11th in importance (0.0083) but 6th in SHAP (1.334), while `com` ranks 4th in importance (0.0951) but only 9th in SHAP (0.650). This suggests the model relies on `via` and `cosd` in interaction-dependent or context-specific ways that raw split-based importance understates, whereas `com`'s high importance may partly reflect correlated redundancy with `pd`/`pv`.

[Design Guidance] The association of high `pd` and `pv` SHAP contributions with the target suggests that compositions optimizing polarization-related descriptors may correlate with favorable dielectric behavior, but this is a model association, not evidence of causality. Any design hypothesis should be tested experimentally, since the nested CV spread (R²=0.7472 ± 0.1632, RMSE=15.4844 ± 6.7395) indicates substantial variability and possible confounding among correlated features like `pd`, `pv`, and `com`.
