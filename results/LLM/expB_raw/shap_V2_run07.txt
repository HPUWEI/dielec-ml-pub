[Key Features] The top features by SHAP mean |value| are `pd` (13.61), `pv` (12.00), and `blm` (2.73), with `pd` and `pv` dominating by roughly 5–10× over the next tier. In dielectric materials, `pd` and `pv` plausibly encode polarization-related descriptors (e.g., polarizability/dipole contributions), while `blm` (B-site electronegativity–Matyonov-Batsanov mean) relates to B-site bonding character and hence ionic/covalent polarization response.

[Model Insight] SHAP and raw importance largely agree at the top (`pd`, `pv`, `blm`), but they diverge for `via` (SHAP rank 4 at 1.89 vs. importance rank 10 at 0.0105), `cosd` (SHAP rank 6 at 1.33 vs. importance rank 11 at 0.0083), and `com` (importance rank 4 at 0.0951 vs. SHAP rank 9 at 0.650). This suggests the model's split-based importance for `com` is inflated by frequent but low-magnitude splits, while `via` and `cosd` carry larger conditional effects, implying interaction or redundancy with the dominant `pd`/`pv`/`blm` features.

[Design Guidance] The association of high `pd` and `pv` SHAP values with the dielectric target suggests that compositions tuning polarization-related descriptors may be worth prioritizing for screening, but this is a model association, not evidence that changing `pd` or `pv` causally improves the dielectric property. Any design hypothesis should be validated experimentally, since the nested CV spread (R²=0.7472 ± 0.1632, RMSE=15.4844 ± 6.7395) indicates substantial uncertainty and possible confounding among correlated features like `pd`, `pv`, `via`, and `cosd`.
