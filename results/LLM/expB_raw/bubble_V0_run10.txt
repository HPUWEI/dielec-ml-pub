[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991). These likely co-vary because pm (A-site/B-site mass-related descriptor), vi, and vm all scale with the same underlying compositional/structural size–mass characteristics of the perovskite unit, so heavier or larger constituent ions shift all three together.

[Redundancy] pm, vm, vi, and m form a tightly overlapping block (pm–vm 0.993, vi–vm 0.991, vi–pm 0.984, pm–m 0.982, vm–m 0.96), and pd–va (r=-0.806) is a strong inverse pair. This indicates substantial shared statistical information among these features, but removal of any one cannot be justified from correlation alone without testing model performance.

[Design Implication] Because the mass/volume block (pm, vm, vi, m) is largely redundant, a compact informative set could pair one representative from that block with complementary, less-correlated descriptors such as the electronegativity/ionization features (Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm, devvm) and the inversely related pd–va pair, which together may capture the chemical and polarizability drivers of the dielectric property.
