[Strong Correlations] The two strongest pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991), followed closely by vi–pm (r=0.984) and pm–m (r=0.982). These co-vary plausibly because pm (A-site/B-site-related mass or molar descriptor), vm (molar volume), vi (ionic volume), and m (mass) are all size/volume/mass descriptors that scale together as the unit-cell contents and cation sizes change across the composition space.

[Redundancy] pm, vm, vi, and m form a tightly overlapping block (all pairwise |r|≥0.96), and pd–va (r=-0.806) is a strong inverse pair; these features carry largely shared statistical information. This does not by itself prove that any one of them can be removed, since each may still contribute unique non-linear signal to the Gradient Boosting model (nested CV R²=0.7472 ± 0.1632, RMSE=15.4844 ± 6.7395).

[Design Implication] Given this structure, a compact informative set would pair one representative from the pm/vm/vi/m size–mass block (e.g., vm) with pd and va to capture the dominant inverse relationship, and then add the chemically distinct descriptors blm, blsd, sm, ssd, com, cosd, Em, Ev, Esd, bvsdm, devvm, via, pa, pv, lsd, asd, d, and GII to supply complementary information for predicting the dielectric property.
