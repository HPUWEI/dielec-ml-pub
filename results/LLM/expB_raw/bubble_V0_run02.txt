[Strong Correlations] The two strongest pairs are pm–vm (r = 0.993) and vi–vm (r = 0.991), followed closely by vi–pm (r = 0.984). These likely co-vary because pm, vm, and vi are all packing/volume-related descriptors—molar volume, unit-cell or molecular volume, and ionic volume—so a larger packing volume tends to accompany a larger molar volume and ionic volume in the same perovskite compositions.

[Redundancy] The cluster {pm, vm, vi, m} carries heavily overlapping statistical information (pm–vm = 0.993, vi–vm = 0.991, vi–pm = 0.984, pm–m = 0.982, vm–m = 0.960), and pd–va (r = −0.806) is a strongly anti-correlated pair. This overlap suggests these features are largely interchangeable in a statistical sense, but correlation alone does not establish that any one of them can definitely be removed without testing its unique contribution to the model.

[Design Implication] Because the volume/packing block {pm, vm, vi, m} is nearly collinear, a model may benefit from retaining one representative from that group (e.g., vm) while pairing it with features from distinct correlation neighborhoods—such as the electronegativity/ionization-energy descriptors (blm, blsd, Em, Ev, Esd, GII) and the anti-correlated pd–va pair—to capture complementary dielectric-relevant information rather than redundant volume signal.
