[Strong Correlations] The two strongest feature pairs are blm–bpv (r = 0.71) and d–bpv (r = −0.733). Materials with longer average bond lengths (blm) tend to have fewer bonds per unit cell volume (bpv), and denser materials (d) pack more bonds into a given volume, so bond length, bond density, and density naturally co-vary through atomic packing and coordination geometry.

[Redundancy] The pairs blm–bpv (0.71), d–bpv (−0.733), d–blm (−0.706), blm–ppv (0.589), and bpv–ppv (0.519) indicate that blm, bpv, d, and ppv share substantial overlapping statistical information. This overlap suggests these features are partially redundant, but removal of any one cannot be justified from correlation alone without checking model importance or ablation performance.

[Design Implication] Because ppv is the most direct physical driver of dielectric constant yet is correlated with blm (0.589) and bpv (0.519), while d is strongly anti-correlated with bpv (−0.733) and blm (−0.706), a combination such as ppv, bg, and FEpa—supplemented by either d or bpv/blm rather than all of them—may capture complementary polarizability, electronic, and stability information for predicting the dielectric property.
