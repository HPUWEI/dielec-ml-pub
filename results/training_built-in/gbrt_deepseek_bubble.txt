[Strong Correlations] The two most strongly correlated pairs are pm–vm (r=0.993) and vi–vm (r=0.991). These likely co-vary because molecular dielectric polarizability (pm), total ionic volume (vi), and unit cell volume (vm) all scale with the number and size of polarizable ions/atoms packed into the cell, so larger cells tend to contain more polarizable ionic volume and yield higher molecular polarizability.

[Redundancy] pm, vi, vm, and m form a tightly overlapping block (pm–vm=0.993, vi–vm=0.991, vi–pm=0.984, pm–m=0.982, vm–m=0.96), and pd–va is strongly inversely related (-0.806). This indicates substantial shared statistical information among these features, though removal of any single feature cannot be justified from correlation alone without validating against model performance.

[Design Implication] For predicting the dielectric property, a feature set combining a polarizability/volume descriptor (e.g., pm or pv) with a packing/ionic-volume descriptor (e.g., pd or va) and a bond-character descriptor (e.g., com or Em) may be informative, since the correlation structure shows polarizability–volume redundancy but also an opposing packing–volume relationship (pd–va=-0.806) that captures distinct structural information.
